## 1. Soft power theory and Japan (set A), for chapters 01 and 06

- A1 (H). Joseph S. Nye Jr., "Soft Power", Foreign Policy No. 80, Autumn 1990, pp. 153-171, DOI 10.2307/1148580. Contrasts co-optive or soft power (getting others to want what you want) with hard or command power; resources are culture, ideology, international institutions. Nye notes Japan's soft power was limited by the inward orientation of its culture. Nye's own pages say he coined the term in the late 1980s, so the 1990 article is the first major publication. Nye lived 1937-2025 (Hachette page).
- A2 (H publisher and definition, M for exact 2004 date). Nye, "Soft Power: The Means to Success in World Politics", PublicAffairs, 2004. Soft power is the ability to attract and persuade, from a country's culture, political ideals, and policies; hard power is coercion from military or economic might.
- A3 (H). Douglas McGray, "Japan's Gross National Cool", Foreign Policy No. 130, May/June 2002, pp. 44-54, DOI 10.2307/3183487. Argument: Japan's cultural influence grew during its economic slump (pop music, electronics, fashion, animation, food), making it more of a cultural superpower than in the 1980s; McGray frames "national cool" as Nye-style soft power and asks whether Japan can use it given its insularity. Cite the 2002 print issue (the foreignpolicy.com copy is a 2009 repost).
- A4 (H). Foreign Minister Taro Aso, speech "A New Look at Cultural Diplomacy: A Call to Japan's Cultural Practitioners", Digital Hollywood University, 28 April 2006. Points: MOFA wants to market pop culture; diplomacy depends on mass public opinion so pop culture should be an ally of diplomacy; creators should help polish the Japan brand; Astro Boy and Doraemon made robots friendly; proposes a manga prize like a Nobel prize for manga and young anime artists as cultural ambassadors; Captain Tsubasa inspired Zidane and Totti.
- A5 (H). Doraemon inaugurated as Anime Ambassador by Foreign Minister Masahiko Koumura at MOFA on 19 March 2008 (MOFA press release, archived; Diplomatic Bluebook 2009). The Japanese title "Anime Bunka Taishi" is U.
- A6 (H). Japan International MANGA Award established May 2007 on the initiative of Foreign Minister Taro Aso, held every year since; the 20th edition took applications in 2026.
- A7 (H). MOFA press release 25 February 2009: three "Trend Communicators of Japanese Pop Culture in the Field of Fashion", nicknamed Kawaii Ambassadors, commissioned 26 February 2009: Misako Aoki (Lolita), Yu Kimura (Harajuku), Shizuka Fujioka (school-uniform style).
- A8 (H). Koichi Iwabuchi, "Pop-culture diplomacy in Japan: soft power, nation branding and the question of 'international cultural exchange'", International Journal of Cultural Policy 21(4), 2015, pp. 419-432, DOI 10.1080/10286632.2015.1042469. Traces postwar cultural diplomacy aimed at softening anti-Japan feeling in Southeast Asia, then soft power, nation branding, and Cool Japan; argues it stays a one-way projection without real cross-border dialogue and sidelines Japan's internal cultural diversity.

## 2. Cool Japan policy (set B), for chapter 07 (and 08, 12)

- B1 (M). METI set up the Creative Industries Promotion Office in June 2010 inside the Manufacturing Industries Bureau to coordinate cultural-industry promotion (design, animation, fashion, film) across agencies (Japan Times editorial, 15 Aug 2010). Exact day U.
- B2 (H). Cool Japan Fund Inc. founded November 2013 as a public-private fund (launch ceremony 25 Nov 2013). Capital at end of FY2023 per Board of Audit: government 123.6 billion yen, private 10.7 billion yen, total 134.3 billion yen (about 92% government). By April 2026: 151.3 billion yen total, 140.6 billion yen from government (nippon.com, 9 Jul 2026).
- B3 (H). Board of Audit report (Reiwa 6): cumulative loss about 39.7 billion yen at end of FY2023; plan targets only 1.0 billion yen cumulative profit by FY2033, far below the roughly 15.0 billion yen cost of the government capital; the Board told the fund to clear losses and improve management. Cumulative losses reached 54 billion yen at end-March 2026; FY2025 net loss 15.7 billion yen; one cause about 14 billion yen in Spiber. Mainichi, 21 Aug 2026, citing government sources: the government decided to dissolve the fund; NHK, 24 Aug 2026: expert panel decision expected around year end. Phrase as "decided, per government sources", not completed.
- B4 (H). New Cool Japan Strategy decided by the Intellectual Property Strategy Headquarters on 4 June 2024: Cool Japan-related industries (content, inbound tourism, food, fashion, cosmetics) to 50 trillion yen or more by 2033 (2023 baseline 19.1 trillion yen). Separate content target: overseas market for Japanese content 20 trillion yen by 2033 (Cabinet Office slides, 3 Oct 2025; IP Promotion Plan 2025).
- B5 (H for 4.7, 5.8 and "above semiconductors"; M for 5.5 and 4.8). Overseas market for Japanese content 4.7 trillion yen in 2022 and 5.8 trillion yen in 2023; the Cabinet Office slide says it exceeded semiconductor exports. Secondary (BBT University, 24 Mar 2026): 2023 semiconductors 5.5 trillion yen, steel 4.8 trillion yen; content second only to autos (21.6 trillion yen). 2024 figure U (sources disagree, 6.0 vs 6.1).
- B6 (H). Yomiuri, 23 Jun 2026: government plans a content-industry "command tower" body within FY2027 modelled on Korea's KOCCA, and about 34 trillion yen of public-private investment by FY2033 (games 24.5, anime 3.3, manga 1.6, music 3 trillion yen). Keidanren, 29 Sep 2026: FY2027 budget requests for content exceed 250 billion yen; urged one support agency. Cabinet approved the Japan Growth Strategy 21 Jul 2026. METI Entertainment and Creative Industries Strategy contents are U.
- B7 (H). J-LOD (Japan content LOcalization and Distribution): METI subsidy run by VIPO that pays part of localisation and promotion costs for overseas release, aiming to create overseas "Japan booms" that lift related industries and inbound tourism.

- C5 (H). Netflix, 7 Jul 2025: over 50% of global members watch anime; anime viewed over 1 billion times on Netflix in 2024, viewership tripled over five years; 80-90% watch dubbed; 33 anime titles reached the Global Top 10 (Non-English) in 2024.

Demon Slayer Mugen Train (Japan release 16 Oct 2020) became Japan's highest-grossing film after 73 days, passing Spirited Away which had held the record for 19 years; first film to pass 40 billion yen in Japan; top-grossing film worldwide in 2020. ## 4. Manga market, digital, piracy (set D), for chapter 09

- D1 (H). Shuppan Kagaku Kenkyujo news release (Feb 2026): 2025 comics market (print plus digital) 692.5 billion yen, down 1.7%, first fall after seven years of growth; print 165.2 billion yen (down 14.0%), digital 527.3 billion yen (up 2.9%) = 76.1% of the market; comics 44.8% of the whole publishing market.
- D2 (H launch and model, M user counts). Shueisha MANGA Plus launched worldwide in January 2019 with 50 titles; new chapters of Weekly Shonen Jump, Jump SQ and Shonen Jump+ series release at the same time as Japan, first and latest chapters free; region limits removed August 2021; 6 million monthly active users in 2023 (ANN, 4 Oct 2023); about 5.5 million monthly active users, nine languages, about 100 titles in 2025 (Asahi, 17 Aug 2025).
- D3 (H). ICv2: US and Canada comics and graphic novel sales 2.075 billion USD in 2021 (up 62%, manga especially strong); 1.94 billion USD in 2024. Circana BookScan via ICv2: manga units Jan-Aug 2023 down 27% from 2022 but up 351% from 2019. Manga plus manhwa 40% of US graphic novel sales in the 12 months to June 2024 (ANN, 14 Sep 2024). Yearly US unit totals U.
- D4 (H 2023, M 2024). France (NielsenIQ-GfK): 2023 manga 39.6 million copies, 331 million EUR, more than 1 in 2 comics bought; 2024 about 35.9 million copies (ActuaLitte, 26 May 2025). Do not call France "second largest".
- D5 (H). Piracy: CODA via PwC estimated 2022 losses at 1.95-2.202 trillion yen (about five times 2019); METI and CODA joint release 26 Jan 2026: about 5.7 trillion yen for digital content in 2025. Manga-mura: launched 2016, offline 17 Apr 2018 after the government asked providers to block it; operator Romi Hoshino extradited from the Philippines in September 2019; Fukuoka District Court (June 2021) sentenced him to three years in prison, a 10 million yen fine and 62 million yen forfeiture; CODA estimated 319.2 billion yen damage Sep 2017-Feb 2018; Tokyo District Court (18 Apr 2024) ordered 1.7 billion yen in damages to Kadokawa, Shueisha and Shogakukan.
- D6 (H). Guinness World Records: One Piece by Eiichiro Oda holds "most copies published for the same comic book series by a single author", 416,566,000 copies (Dec 1997 to Jul 2022); Guinness later reported 500 million copies printed worldwide. Keep the two numbers separate.
- D7 (M). Japan Times, 5 May 2019: Korean webtoons put Japan's print manga "on notice"; young digital-native readers move to app-based titles.

## 5. History and industry model (set E), for chapters 02-05

- E1 (H). Tezuka Productions (the official site "Tezuka Osamu Official" of creator Osamu Tezuka, whose 1960s timeline records his own March 1963 trip to sign with NBC): Tetsuwan Atom (Astro Boy) by Mushi Production aired on Fuji TV from 1 Jan 1963 to 31 Dec 1966, 193 episodes, first Japanese 30-minute animated TV series, peak rating 40.7%, aired in America, China and Europe. NBC Enterprises detail: see gap wave W3.
- E2 (H). Marc Steinberg, "Anime's Media Mix: Franchising Toys and Characters in Japan", University of Minnesota Press, 2012: media mix (Japan) is what the West calls convergence; both "ignited" on 1 Jan 1963 with Astro Boy, which Meiji Seika chocolate marketing turned into a character franchise (stickers); the same publisher page (set E report) states that Meiji Seika chocolates sponsored the show; line from Astro Boy to Pokemon, Doraemon, Hello Kitty.
- E3 (M). Sakuga Blog, 2 May 2017: production committee (seisaku iinkai) is a joint venture of several companies to finance a work; it spreads risk (manageable losses, smaller share of a hit) and joins specialists; larger investors appear higher in credits; the animation studio is often a minor member or not a member; Akira was an early committee film (Kodansha, MBS, Bandai, Hakuhodo, Toho, Tokyo Movie and others); the model spread after Evangelion (1995).
- E4 (M, see gap W2). Akira released in Japan 16 Jul 1988; North American limited release by Streamline Pictures 25 Dec 1989; international cult following; widely cited as influential.
- E5 (H dates). The Pokemon Company history page: TV anime starts on TV Tokyo April 1997; Mewtwo Strikes Back opens in Japan July 1998; anime launches in the US September 1998 on 111 stations, and Red/Blue launch in the US; Pokemon The First Movie launches in the US November 1999. Smithsonian Magazine (27 Feb 2026): first Pokemon games released 27 Feb 1996. Box office (M, Box Office Mojo via Wikipedia): opened at number one in North America, 85.7 million USD North America, 172 million USD worldwide. Franchise total revenue is an estimate only; do not state as official.
- E6 (M, see gap W4). Dragon Ball 260 million copies worldwide (Toei, 15 Jun 2022), Naruto 250 million (Oricon, 13 Apr 2023).
- E7 (H). Sean Leonard, "Progress against the law: Anime and fandom, with the key to the globalization of culture", International Journal of Cultural Studies 8(3), Sep 2005, pp. 281-305, DOI 10.1177/1367877905055679. Argues fans' continual copyright infringement 1976-1993 spurred commerce and the arts; fan proselytizing ignited the US anime movement while Japanese rights holders had abandoned the American market; idea of "cultural sinks"; since 1998 most fansubs are digital files. Scanlation claims are U.

## 7. Limits, labor, tourism, competition (set G), for chapters 10 and 12

- G1 (H). ANN (17 Jan 2026): survey of 130 companies and 165 freelancers; 52.1% of animators unsatisfied with pay; problems include delayed or reduced payment, unpaid retakes, one-sided terms.
- G4 (H Kamakura, M list). Anime Tourism Association publishes "88 Japanese Anime Spots" yearly (2027 edition voting closed 20 Sep 2026). - G5 (H). Inbound visitors: more than 36.8 million in 2024 (AFP via France24, 15 Jan 2025, JNTO estimate); 42.7 million in 2025 with visitor spending 9.5 trillion yen (nippon.com, 23 Jan 2026, JNTO preliminary); 2030 targets 60 million visitors and 15 trillion yen.
